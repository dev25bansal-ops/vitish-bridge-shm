##### PHYSICS  (12 projects) #####
KEY TECHNIQUES:
  - Euler-Bernoulli beam modal analysis: for a simply supported span, f_n = (n^2*pi/(2L^2))*sqrt(EI/(rho*A)) — the single most useful closed-form relation (fundamental frequency f1 directly yields stiffness once mass/length are known)
  - Direct-stiffness FE core: 2D frame/truss element matrices (EA/L axial + 6-DOF bending), consistent mass matrix, solve generalized eigenvalue problem K*phi = lambda*M*phi for frequencies + mode shapes; MAC = |phi_m^T phi_fe|^2/(|phi_m|^2|phi_fe|^2) to correlate measured vs model modes at sensor DOFs
  - Operational modal analysis: Welch-PSD averaging + peak picking (fastest), FDD or covariance SSI (robust, via pyOMA2) on a live accelerometer stream; output-only, no known input needed
  - Stiffness-from-frequency inversion (health index): for a simply supported span EI = 4*f1^2*L^4*rho*A/pi^2; track EI drift over time (cracking/spalling lowers effective EI) — a single-line demo health metric
  - Sensitivity-based FE model updating (Gauss-Newton): assemble residuals of measured-vs-modeled frequencies + MAC, d(f_i)/d(theta) = (1/(2*omega_i)) * phi_i^T (dK/dtheta - omega_i^2 dM/dtheta) phi_i (mass-normalized), update EI per span / boundary spring stiffness; ~20-50 lines of numpy for a small model
  - Bayesian FE model updating (full-fidelity): posterior over stiffness params via asymptotic Gaussian or MCMC (Dowling Hall style) — gives uncertainty on damage, not just a point update

##### DATA  (10 projects) #####
KEY TECHNIQUES:
  - SENSOR NOISE FLOORS & SAMPLING RATES (implement directly). Accelerometers: sample 100-200 Hz (Nyquist comfortably covers the 1st 5-10 modes: long-span fundamental 0.1-3 Hz, short-span 3-10 Hz); SHM-grade force-balance (e.g., Kinemetrics EpiSensor) noise ~0.5-10 micro-g RMS; low-noise MEMS (ADXL355) ~25 micro-g/sqrt(Hz) ~= 0.2-0.4 mg RMS over 100-200 Hz; consumer MEMS (ADXL345/MPU6050) ~150-300 micro-g/sqrt(Hz) ~= 1.5-4 mg RMS. Ambient deck vibration is 1-100 milli-g RMS, so MEMS SNR is believable only above ~1 mg. Strain gauges: sample 50-100 Hz for dynamic (rainflow needs true peaks) or 1 Hz for slow; foil/VW strain resolution 0.5-1 microstrain, traffic-induced strain 10-30 microstrain, daily thermal strain in concrete +-50-100 microstrain, yield ~2000 microstrain. Displacement: LVDT/wire 0.01 mm at 10-20 Hz; RTK-GPS 1-2 cm at 10-20 Hz; total station ~1 mm. Temperature: 0.01-0.1 Hz (sample every 10-60 s). Wind: 10-20 Hz for gust/buffeting, 10-min means for standards. WIM: event-based per vehicle, B-WIM strain sampled 100-200 Hz. Ref: Ragam & Sahebraoji 2019 (IET WSS, DOI 10.1049/iet-wss.2018.5099); Siringoringo et al. 2019 (DOI 10.12783/shm2019/32394).
  - ADDING REALISTIC NOISE TO A SYNTHETIC STREAM (formula-level). Model every channel as y(t) = h(x(t)) + eps(t) + drift(t) + spikes(t), where eps = white Gaussian with sigma from the sensor datasheet (e.g., 0.3 mg RMS accel, 2 microstrain strain), drift = AR(1) random walk with sigma of a few microstrain/hour for strain and sub-mg for accel (thermal/baseline drift), spikes = impulsive Poisson arrivals (~0.1% of samples), dropout = random 0.1-1% missing messages to simulate RF/MQTT loss, plus 16-bit ADC quantization over the range (e.g., +-2 g -> 61 micro-g step). Physical calibration: add a gain/scale-factor per channel, a static offset, and a temperature coefficient (strain gauge alpha ~10-12 microstrain/C). Report per-channel sigma, sampling rate, and filter in a data-realism manifest so reviewers can audit. Ref: Birnie & Ravasi 2021 (DOI 10.3997/2214-4609.202112682); Hagn & Grau 2021 (DOI 10.1145/3488904.3493383).
  - TRAFFIC LOADING MODEL - EUROCODE LM1 / AASHTO HL-93 (concrete values). Eurocode EN 1991-2 LM1: Lane 1 tandem 300 kN (2 x 150 kN axles, 1.2 m apart) + UDL 9 kN/m2; Lane 2 tandem 200 kN + UDL 2.5 kN/m2; Lanes 3+ tandem 100 kN + 2.5 kN/m2; alpha factors adjust per country. LM2 = single 400 kN axle (frequent heavy axle), LM3 = special vehicles (heavy indivisible loads), LM4 = crowd. AASHTO LRFD HL-93: design truck HS20-44 = 8 kip front + 32 kip + 32 kip (14-30 ft spacing, ~36 t GVW), design tandem = 2 x 25 kip at 4 ft, design lane load 0.64 klf, dynamic load allowance 33% (truck/tandem only, not lane load). To simulate realistically, generate a truck event (GVW from a distribution, axle spacings from vehicle class, speed), compute its influence-line response on the deck, and add the dynamic amplification. Refs: EN 1991-2:2003; AASHTO LRFD Bridge Design Specifications sec. 3.6.1.2.
  - TRUCK-COUNT / GVW / VEHICLE-CLASS MODEL (implementable). Trucks arrive as a Poisson process (exponential inter-arrival times) with hourly mean lambda set from an AADT split: motorway AADT 20,000-50,000 with 10-20% heavy trucks; low-traffic rural bridge AADT 1,000-5,000 with 5-15% trucks. Assign vehicle class per FHWA 13-class scheme (dominant Class 9 = 3-S2 5-axle tractor-semitrailer, then Class 8, 13, etc.) and draw GVW from a class-specific bimodal histogram: European 5-axle peak ~30-33 t (near the 40 t legal limit) with a secondary empty/part-load mode ~10-15 t; US 5-axle peak near 80,000 lb.   - WIM ACCURACY & DATA QUALITY (numbers to quote). ##### DEGRADATION  (18 projects) #####
KEY TECHNIQUES:
  - MARKOV DETERIORATION MODEL (DEMO: YES — the centerpiece). Represent the bridge as element condition states (CoRe/SNBI 1-4 or NBI 0-9) with a row-stochastic transition matrix P; the state distribution evolves p_{t+1}=p_t·P and expected time-to-state-exceedance = sum of first-passage expectations. A 4-state matrix is ~30 lines of numpy. Use published transition rates (Cesare 1992; Golabi/Shepard 1997 Pontis; state-DOT fits IL/IN/NY) as priors and label the curve 'illustrative, literature-based priors'.
  - BAYESIAN UPDATING OF THE DETERIORATION CURVE (DEMO: YES — the honest 'data-driven curve'). Treat each measured crack-derived condition state as an observation and update the posterior over transition rates (conjugate Dirichlet-multinomial is fully implementable; the J. Bridge Eng 2020 method is the rigorous version). This is the defensible mechanism for 'deterioration curve driven by the measured crack index' — you update beliefs, you do NOT claim a calibrated fleet curve.
  - IMAGE-BASED CONDITION RATING / CRACK WIDTH → CONDITION STATE (DEMO: YES — the driver, already 80% built). Run the real crack segmentation, measure crack area ratio and max width via skeleton+distance transform on the mask, and map to severity classes (hairline <0.3 mm, medium 0.3-1 mm, wide >1 mm) and to element condition states. This mirrors how inspectors' crack measurements set CoRe/SNBI states and IQOA classes in practice (France IQOA; AASHTO CoRe; FHWA crack-width criteria; ACI 224R flexural crack-width limits 0.004-0.006 in).
  - BRIDGE HEALTH INDEX (DEMO: YES — single-number health score). BHI = 100·Σ(w_i·state_i)/Σ(w_i·state_max) over element condition states (FHWA-HRT-15-081 synthesis). Fully computable from the crack-derived states; gives the dashboard a believable 0-100 health number that moves with each inspection/MQTT update.
  - NBI/SNBI COMPONENT RATING + SUFFICIENCY (DEMO: YES — the condition card). Map the crack index to deck/super/substructure 0-9 and to Good/Fair/Poor, flag 'structurally deficient' at ≤4, and compute the sufficiency-style weighting. All table lookups; the twin's condition panel mirrors FHWA practice.
  - PARIS-LAW / LEFM FATIGUE CRACK GROWTH (DEMO: CONDITIONAL YES — needs a stress-range history). Implement da/dN = C·ΔK^m, ΔK = Y·Δσ·√(πa), integrate to cycles-to-critical. Credible only if you state an assumed initial crack size, ΔK_th and geometry factor Y, and feed measured (strain/rainflow) or clearly-simulated stress ranges. NCHRP 721 / FHWA fatigue evaluation give categories and CAFL values. Without NDE crack sizing it is illustrative, not a real steel-crack forecast.
